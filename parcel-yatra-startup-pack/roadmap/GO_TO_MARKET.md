# Go To Market

**Status:** Draft v0.1 · **Date:** 2026-10-24
**Constraint:** no paid acquisition at pilot. If 20 users require advertising, the proposition is not
yet proven and money will not fix that.

---

## 1. The positioning, in one paragraph

> Parcel Yatra matches someone who needs to send a small, sealed parcel between two cities with a
> verified traveller who is already making that journey by car. You don't wait for a courier network,
> and you don't hand your documents to a stranger without a record — every handover is OTP-verified
> and photographed. We only carry documents and small low-value goods, we don't insure them yet, and
> we say so.

That is the whole pitch. If it needs a second paragraph, it is not clear enough.

## 2. Who this is for (and who it is not)

| For | Not for |
|---|---|
| Someone in Bareilly who needs property papers in Delhi this week | Anyone shipping 50 kg of goods |
| A student sending certificates home | Anyone sending cash, jewellery or medicine |
| A small business sending samples to a client | Anyone who needs a same-day guarantee |
| A traveller already driving to Delhi who wants their fuel cost covered a little | Anyone who wants to run a courier business |
| People who currently ask a relative or a bus conductor | Enterprise logistics buyers |

Naming who it is **not** for is the fastest way to be understood. It also filters out the support
conversations that would sink a two-person team.

## 3. The wedge

Entering against established logistics is a losing move. The wedge is:

**"The thing you already do informally — asking a cousin or a bus driver to carry an envelope — with a
record, a verified person, and a code at both ends."**

This is not a new behaviour. It is an existing, slightly unsafe behaviour with a thin layer of trust on
top. That is a much easier sell than convincing someone to switch couriers.

## 4. Channel plan (pilot, in priority order)

### 4.1 Warm network (weeks 1–2)

- Direct, individual conversations. Not a broadcast to a WhatsApp group.
- Ask for one specific action: "Do you have anything you need to send to Delhi in the next two weeks?"
- Expected: the first 5–10 bookings.

### 4.2 Physical presence where documents actually move (weeks 2–6)

| Location | Why |
|---|---|
| Stationery and printing shops near courts | People print, sign, and courier documents here |
| College notice boards and student groups | Certificates, admit cards, documents home |
| Small coaching centres | Admission documents to Delhi/Lucknow |

Approach: talk to the shop owner, not the wall. A shopkeeper who understands the product will mention
it to the next person who asks "where can I courier this?".

### 4.3 Traveller-first referral (weeks 3+)

A traveller who has had a good experience is the best recruiter of both sides:
- They recruit other travellers (they know who drives to Delhi).
- They recruit senders (their own contacts ask "you're going to Delhi?").

No cash referral at pilot. A cash incentive buys the wrong travellers.

### 4.4 Local language content (weeks 2+)

- Hindi-first landing page, written as Hindi, not translated.
- A single explainer image that works as a WhatsApp forward. Not a video.
- One page answering: "Is this safe? What if it gets lost? Who is this traveller?"

### 4.5 Channels deliberately not used

| Channel | Why not |
|---|---|
| Paid Instagram/Meta ads | Expensive, wrong intent, unmeasurable at this scale |
| Influencer marketing | Buys attention, not trust, in this category |
| App store optimisation | Nobody searches for this by name yet |
| Cold email to businesses | Not the pilot user; enterprise sales is a different motion |
| PR / "startup coverage" | Attracts competitors and regulators before the model is proven |
| Discount codes | Trains users to wait for discounts on a ₹900 product |

## 5. The trust problem, addressed directly

The core objection is "why would I give my documents to a stranger?" Every channel must answer it the
same way, in the same order:

1. **The traveller is verified** — ID checked by a human, not an automated pass.
2. **The handover is recorded** — OTP at pickup, OTP at delivery, photo at both ends.
3. **The platform knows who both parties are** — and can be called.
4. **The scope is deliberately small** — documents and low-value goods only, because we cannot yet
   insure more. This is honesty used as a feature.

What we must **not** say: "100% safe", "insured", "guaranteed", "police verified", "government
approved". Each is false, and one false claim destroys the only asset this business has.

## 6. Pricing communication

- Show the breakdown, always: traveller's share, platform fee, tax.
- Say what the traveller earns. Senders are more comfortable when they know the person carrying their
  parcel is being paid fairly.
- Never use a fake "was ₹1,200, now ₹899".
- If the price changes, say so and re-confirm. The quote expiry exists for this reason.

Pricing itself is a hypothesis (`docs/PRICING_AND_UNIT_ECONOMICS.md`); all figures there are marked as
such and none should be published as a fact until the pilot measures real conversion.

## 7. Launch sequence

| Week | Activity | Success signal |
|---|---|---|
| −2 | Recruit 10–15 travellers; verify each manually | 10 verified travellers with ≥ 2 planned trips |
| −1 | Walk 5 senders through the app in person; fix the top 3 confusions | 5 senders who can complete a booking unaided |
| 0 | First 10 parcels, high-touch | ≥ 8 delivered; notes from every session |
| 1–2 | Open to the warm network only | ≥ 50% posted→confirmed |
| 3–4 | Physical channel (shops, colleges) | Inbound from outside the network |
| 5–6 | Evaluate against exit criteria | Go / iterate / stop decision |

**Do not compress week −2.** Supply is the constraint, and a sender who finds no traveller does not
come back for a second attempt.

## 8. Messaging by audience

### Sender
> Send it with someone already going.
> Verified traveller. Code at pickup. Code at delivery. Photo both times.
> Documents and small parcels only. Not insured yet — we'll tell you exactly what that means.

### Traveller
> You're already driving. Carry a small sealed parcel. Get paid after it's delivered.
> You choose what you accept. You can refuse anything that looks wrong, with no penalty.

### Receiver
> Rohit is sending you a parcel from Bareilly.
> Tap this link when the traveller arrives. You'll need the 6-digit code we texted you.

### Local shopkeeper (referral partner)
> If a customer asks you where to courier documents to Delhi, tell them about this. It's cheaper than
> a courier and a real person carries it.

Each message names one benefit and one limitation. The limitation is not a weakness in the copy — it
is what makes the benefit believable.

## 9. What "traction" means here (and what it does not)

| Claim | Honest status after a 20-parcel pilot |
|---|---|
| "We have product-market fit" | **No.** 20 parcels is a smoke test |
| "Users love it" | Only if measured — otherwise, an anecdote |
| "The market is X crore" | Unverified; no primary research has been done |
| "We're growing 40% week over week" | Meaningless at n=5 |
| "The flow works end to end" | **Yes**, if 20 parcels moved |
| "People will hand over documents to a verified stranger" | **Yes**, if conversion held |

Use the second column. It is less impressive and it is true, and it is what makes the next conversation
worth having.

## 10. Competitive positioning (based on observed public information only)

`research/COMPETITOR_LANDSCAPE.md` records what was actually observed. Summary of the honest position:

| Player | What they do (observed) | Where we differ |
|---|---|---|
| Porter | On-demand intra-city logistics, 2W and trucks, plus Packers & Movers | We are inter-city and traveller-based, not fleet-based; we are not competing for intra-city delivery |
| Kootr | Air-traveller parcel carrying, "PIN-verified handover", India-wide claim | We are car-based and corridor-focused; we do not claim national coverage |

**What we do not know:** whether either is profitable, their volume, their unit economics, or whether
their claimed coverage is real. No financial or operational data was available.

**What we must not do:** copy their copy, their assets, or their prohibited-items list verbatim (theirs
is their policy, not Indian law).

## 11. Competitive moat (candid version)

At pilot there is no moat. What could become one:

1. **Corridor density** — being the default for Bareilly⇄Delhi, not "India-wide".
2. **Traveller reliability data** — accumulated acceptance and completion history.
3. **Operational trust** — a support number that a person actually answers. Hard to copy at scale,
   easy to lose.
4. **Local brand** — being known in one city is achievable; being known nationally is not, with this
   budget.

Anything claiming a technology moat is wrong. The matching is a query.

## 12. Regulatory posture in marketing

- Do not describe the traveller as a courier or an employee.
- Do not use the word "escrow" or imply we hold funds.
- Do not claim insurance.
- Do not advertise modes we are not permitted to operate.
- Say "pilot" where it is a pilot.

These are not legal footnotes; they are the difference between a defensible business and a misleading
one.

| Item | Approach | Cash |
|---|---|---|
| Acquisition | Zero paid media | ₹0 |
| Print (small posters, shop cards) | Minimal, local | Small |
| SMS | Real users only | Small |

| Legal | **The real cost.** Budget for counsel; do not skip it | Substantial and unavoidable |

The cheapest part of this launch is the software. The most expensive is the legal work, and it is the
part that determines whether the business exists.

## 14. The one-paragraph summary for an outsider

> We're testing whether people in Bareilly will trust a verified traveller with a sealed envelope
> instead of a courier. Two corridors, twenty parcels, no real money moving yet, and an explicit
> promise that nothing is insured. If it works, we'll harden the operations before we take a rupee. If
> it doesn't, we'll know in six weeks and it will have cost very little.

That is the honest position of this pack today.
